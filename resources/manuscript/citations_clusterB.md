# Cluster B — §3 Benchmarking against peer biobanks

Verified seed citations. Every entry below was confirmed against a real source (PubMed, PMC, publisher, or a resolvable DOI) with title + first author + year matching. Do not add unverified references.

### §3.1 The comparator set (cohort-profile / foundational papers)

- [Sudlow et al. UK Biobank: an open access resource. PLoS Medicine 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25826379/) — Canonical UK Biobank cohort-profile paper (500,000 participants, aged 40–69, open-access model); the primary comparator.
- [Bycroft et al. The UK Biobank resource with deep phenotyping and genomic data. Nature 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0579-z) — Describes the genome-wide genotyping, imputation (~96M variants) and QC that made UK Biobank the template for genomics-at-scale.
- [Gaziano et al. Million Veteran Program: a mega-biobank. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26441289/) — Foundational design paper for the VA Million Veteran Program, an EHR-linked health-system biobank (a key non-European-enriched comparator).
- [Kurki et al. FinnGen provides genetic insights from a well-phenotyped isolated population. Nature 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36653562/) — Flagship FinnGen paper linking ~429k biobank samples to lifelong national health registers; the founder-population comparator.
- [Chen et al. China Kadoorie Biobank of 0.5 million people. International Journal of Epidemiology 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22158673/) — Cohort-profile paper for CKB (512,891 adults, 10 regions), the large non-Western comparator.

### §3.2 Distinctive strengths and scientific output (discovery at scale, diversity of approaches, cross-biobank)

- [Backman et al. Exome sequencing and analysis of 454,787 UK Biobank participants. Nature 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04103-z) — Landmark biobank-scale exome resource (coding variants across ~4,000 traits); exemplifies sequencing-driven discovery layered onto a biobank.
- [Halldorsson et al. The sequences of 150,119 genomes in the UK Biobank. Nature 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35859178/) — Biobank-scale whole-genome sequencing (585M SNPs, structural variants, microsatellites); benchmark for the WGS resource AoU also delivers.
- [Verma et al. Diversity and scale: genetic architecture of 2,068 traits in the VA Million Veteran Program. Science 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adj1182) — Multi-ancestry GWAS atlas across 635,969 MVP participants; 1,608 loci only significant once non-European groups were included — the discovery case for diverse biobanks.
- [Wojcik et al. Genetic analyses of diverse populations improves discovery for complex traits. Nature 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6785182/) — PAGE-study demonstration that ancestrally diverse samples yield variants missed in European-only studies; the scientific argument for AoU-style diversity.
- [Zhou et al. Global Biobank Meta-analysis Initiative: powering genetic discovery across human disease. Cell Genomics 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9903716/) — Cross-biobank comparison framework (23 biobanks, 4 continents, >2.2M individuals); the reference for harmonizing and comparing biobanks.

### §3.3 Gaps and limitations (diversity, representativeness, generalizability)

- [Sirugo et al. The Missing Diversity in Human Genetic Studies. Cell 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30901543/) — Widely cited statement of the ancestry imbalance (as of 2018, ~78% of GWAS participants European) and its scientific/equity costs.
- [Martin et al. Clinical use of current polygenic risk scores may exacerbate health disparities. Nature Genetics 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-019-0379-x) — Shows PRS are far more accurate in European-ancestry individuals; core evidence that Eurocentric biobanks limit equitable prediction.
- [Popejoy & Fullerton. Genomics is failing on diversity. Nature 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/538161a) — Early high-profile audit quantifying non-European underrepresentation in the GWAS Catalog; frames the gap AoU set out to close.
- [Fry et al. Comparison of sociodemographic and health-related characteristics of UK Biobank participants with those of the general population. American Journal of Epidemiology 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28641372/) — Documents UK Biobank's 5.5% response rate and "healthy-volunteer" selection bias; the representativeness caveat for all mega-biobanks.
- [Mills & Rahal. A scientometric review of genome-wide association studies. Communications Biology 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-018-0261-x) — Bibliometric analysis of 3,639 GWAS showing persistent European dominance (88% in 2017) and concentration in three countries; the biobank/GWAS bibliometrics anchor.

### §3.4 Interpretation (why AoU benchmarks against these cohorts)

- [All of Us Research Program Investigators. The "All of Us" Research Program. New England Journal of Medicine 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsr1809937) — Foundational AoU design paper stating the diversity-first rationale (>80% from underrepresented groups) that motivates benchmarking against the comparator set above.

### Caveats

- The AoU NEJM 2019 program paper (Denny et al. / All of Us Research Program Investigators) is the *subject* program, not a peer comparator; it is included here only for the §3.4 benchmarking rationale and likely also belongs to another cluster — de-duplicate before merging.
- For China Kadoorie Biobank there is also an earlier predecessor profile (Chen et al., "Cohort profile: the Kadoorie Study of Chronic Disease in China (KSCDC)," Int J Epidemiol 2005;34:1243–9). The 2011 paper above is the canonical 0.5-million-person profile and is used here; the 2005 paper was not separately fetched/verified and is omitted.
