All of Us Scientific Roadmap Refresh — Executive Summary

2026 Science Committee Working Group · Co-leads: Jordan Smoller & Melissa Haendel · Draft 2026-06-02

The question

The Science Committee asked WG-1 to refresh the All of Us Scientific Roadmap against the Roadmap 2.0 six questions: what has changed since 2023; the largest data gaps; reproducibility expectations; new tools needed; what exposures and outcomes to capture; and whether the 8 priority focus areas are still right. Our charge is uniquely structural — only this WG can change the framework the other four 2026 WGs must fit inside.

The evidence base

We analyzed 1,428 substantive AoU publications and 12,899 workspace projects, theme-tagged against a locked 19-theme taxonomy. We crosswalked themes onto the 8 focus areas and computed pre/post-2023 trends. We extracted 166 strategic priorities from 29 NIH strategic plans (NIH-Wide + 21 ICs) and built an alignment matrix. We compared AoU against 4 peer biobanks (UK Biobank 11,868, FinnGen 3,152, MVP 438, China Kadoorie 558 — full PubMed censuses) using a same-tagger approach. We also ran an open-discovery horizon scan of 294k preprints + 423k NIH grants (2021–2026) for emerging white-space. Recommendations were scored against the 8-criterion Sci Com rubric Smoller established in November 2024.

Headline findings

  • The 8-area framework holds at the top level but has three structural problems: a Methods/AI orphan (18% of pubs, no home), wearables orphaned despite being a slide-7 priority, and multi-omics invisible inside “Genetics & Biology.”
  • Strongest IC alignments: NHGRI×Genomics (largest single matrix cell; AoU delivers Bold Prediction #9), NIMHD×SDOH (Goal 7 literally describes the AoU mandate), NLM×Methods (the only IC plan that explicitly names AoU).
  • Biggest demand-side gaps: NIEHS×Environment (6 IC priorities vs. 8 AoU pubs — the single largest gap); imaging across NCI/NEI/NIBIB (data-supply, not demand); multi-omics for NIDDK/NHLBI (the 2026 omics releases should close this mechanically).
  • Peer-biobank comparison (full-census peers): AoU is under-indexed ~4× on rare disease, ~6× on multi-omics, ~13× vs UKB on imaging, near peer median on aging/ADRD — but leads peers 26× on ELSI, 5× on SDOH, 4.6× on PGx, 3× on methods (distinctive ≠ growing — AoU’s defensible identity).
  • Horizon scan — emerging white-space: fastest-rising AoU-feasible gaps are genomic foundation models and LLMs/agentic AI (AoU <0.3%; LLM-in-medicine up 47× since 2021), plus MASLD adoption — mapping onto the 9th focus area and AI co-scientist. Single-cell/spatial omics & AI protein structure are watch-not-build (outside AoU’s data model).
  • Pan-NIH triangulation: five plans articulate explicit pan-IC infrastructure demand (NIH-Wide Objective 1; NLM Goal 1; NHGRI Bold Predictions #5/#9; NIMHD Goal 7; NIH-Wide CCT4/CCT5) — empirical basis for an AoU-as-pan-NIH-infrastructure framing.
  • Four slide-7 priorities are already realized (AI 33 pubs; breakthrough therapeutics 20; wearables 75, mature; G×E×SDOH 11); four are aspirational pending upstream investment (multi-omics, imaging, microbiome, environmental).

Caveats

  • Dermatology over-representation (13.9% of corpus, peer index 6.9×) is an artifact driven by 1–2 prolific groups; trend is declining (−4.4). Do not treat as a strategic priority.
  • Peer-biobank counts use PubMed name-search fallback (same heuristic across peers); use the static share index, not trend differentials, for action-relevant comparisons.
  • AoU pre-2023 baseline is tiny (157 pubs); apparent “growth” on cardiometabolic/mental health/cancer is a small-baseline artifact — read as “presence built,” not “outpacing the field.”
