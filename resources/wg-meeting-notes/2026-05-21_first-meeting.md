## Overview from Geoff

**New developments since 2023 to potentially incorporate:**

- Multi-omics for diagnostics
- Wearables as real-world continuous data
- Imaging + multimodal combinations for risk prediction
- AI/agents across the road map
- Combinatorial risk scores (polygenic + clinical + environmental)
- Novel therapeutics (e.g., GLP-1s)

**Start with what questions we want to ask, then identify what data is needed — not the other way around.**

## Reproducibility & shared artifacts

- Phenotype definitions (e.g., Type 2 diabetes) are inconsistent across AoU
  publications, creating noise.
- Need a curated, versioned, attributable "gallery" of reusable computable
  phenotypes and other artifacts with community vetting/upvoting.

## Data gaps & clinical notes

- EHR data alone is insufficient for many goals; clinical notes are
  underutilized and appear very promising (e.g., earlier lupus detection — paper
  forthcoming from Stephanie).
- Tension between what is in the published literature and what big data makes
  possible.

## Research-to-clinical implementation gap

- Research pipelines and clinical-implementation pipelines are completely siloed.
- AoU's scale and care-network data sourcing could help bridge this and improve
  data equity.

## AI / agents

- Significant enthusiasm for agentic workflows: automated phenotyping, target-
  trial-emulation pipelines, foundation models trained on AoU data, multimodal
  integration support.

## IC alignment

- Engaging NIH Institutes (ICs) is important but the LCT group no longer exists.
- Melissa proposed using Claude to analyze IC strategic plans, compare to
  literature trends, and diff against AoU publication topics. → became the
  `aou-ic-alignment/` evidence base.
