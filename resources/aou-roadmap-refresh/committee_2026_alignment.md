# New ideas from the 2026 Science Committee meeting series

> **Added 2026-10-14.** The AoU Science Committee's 2026 meeting series surfaced
> several program directions that post-date our original analysis and that the
> roadmap refresh should reflect. This note captures the **program-level, publicly
> citable** ideas only; candid internal discussion and unpublished specifics are
> deliberately excluded. (The committee's five 2026 workgroups — Roadmap 2.0,
> Translational Science, Imaging Integration, AI Strategies, and Scientific
> Communications — are the same "need areas" this analysis already addresses.)

## 1. The "driver project / driver paper" model  *(new structural idea)*
The **Women's Health Driver Project** (Feb 2026) demonstrated how the program's
**structured biostatistical support** can produce exemplar analyses that both deliver
science and serve as a reusable template. The committee explicitly framed it as a
**model for driver papers on other conditions — including rare diseases and
*intersectional* conditions (e.g., rare disease + rurality).**
- **Roadmap implication:** adopt driver projects as a standing mechanism, and make
  **rare-disease and intersectional (equity × rare-disease × geography) driver
  papers** an explicit near-term deliverable — this directly attacks the rare-disease
  peer-gap (see `../peer-biobanks/underrepresented_vs_peers.md`) using AoU's
  distinctive diversity.
- Source: AoU Women's Health Driver Project; paper forthcoming, *Journal of Women's
  Health* (verify citation before manuscript use).

## 2. Generalizability analysis + tooling  *(new, operationalized)*
An RTI/NIH/Northwestern **Driver Project** produced methods **and a released tool —
the `waou` R package** — for **survey-weighting All of Us estimates to be
generalizable to the US population**, addressing the program's volunteer/ascertainment
bias.
- **Roadmap implication:** this operationalizes the "ascertainment-bias-adjusted
  variables / generalizability" need already flagged in the data-types inventory —
  promote `waou`-style weighting to a **first-class, documented Workbench capability**
  and a reusable research asset.
- Source: "Assessing the generalizability of prevalence estimates from the All of Us
  Research Program," *American Journal of Epidemiology* (2026); `waou` R package (CRAN)
  (verify citation/version before manuscript use).

## 3. Multi-omics on 10,000 diverse participants  *(live program activity)*
The AoU **Multi-Omics Working Group** is generating **RNA-seq + plasma proteomics
(Olink Explorer HT) + PacBio long-read WGS** on ~10,000 participants selected to
reflect cohort diversity (~1/3 highly admixed), with discussion of **scaling toward
~50,000**. The program's diversity makes this uniquely powerful for **ancestry-aware**
molecular-trait and rare-variant work.
- **Roadmap implication:** confirms the multi-omics/proteomics-release recommendation
  (rename FA8 to "Genetics, Multi-omics & Biology") as a *live activity*, not just an
  ask; and strengthens the case for **QTL-informed polygenic risk scores** and
  ancestry-fair functional-genomics methods.

## 4. External biospecimen-request mechanism  *(new, publicly posted)*
The program has **approved and publicly posted a mechanism for external investigators
— academic, nonprofit, and industry — to request AoU biospecimens (plasma, serum, DNA)
to generate additional data types.**
- **Roadmap implication:** this is the data-generation counterpart to a partner
  ecosystem and converges with the ACT-IAC AI/ML white paper's **Research Innovation
  Partner Ecosystem** and our RW "reusable assets / partner tools" gap — the roadmap
  should connect biospecimen-based data generation to a graduated partner-contribution
  and reusable-asset model.

## 5. Imaging ingestion strategy  *(enriches the imaging data-type rec)*
The committee's imaging workgroup flagged that **legacy/opportunistic imaging is
low-yield** (a large share of legacy neuroimaging is unusable for quality/equipment/
sequence reasons) and that **imaging statistical expertise and standardized
QA/harmonization are needed upfront**, with **RSNA** and **MIDRC** as potential
partners and **incidental-findings return** as a governance question. UK Biobank's
research-purpose imaging (≈100k images → ≈1,300 publications) is the productivity
benchmark.
- **Roadmap implication:** sharpen the imaging data-type recommendation
  (`../deliverables/data_types_inventory.html`) toward **research-purpose, QA-harmonized
  acquisition with partners (RSNA/MIDRC) and an incidental-findings policy**, not bulk
  ingestion of legacy scans.

## 6. Long-read analysis (Phase 1 + Phase 2)  *(reinforces rare-disease substrate)*
Dedicated long-read (PacBio) WGS analysis efforts reinforce AoU's long-read substrate
for rare/Mendelian and structural-variant discovery — the clearest addressable
rare-disease opportunity in the peer comparison.
