# WG-1: Scientific Roadmap Refresh

> This is a **topic / need area**, not a decision body. Its topic overlaps
> with the other need areas and should be coordinated across them. The WG
> label is used here as a topic reference.

## Lead(s) and scope

**Co-leads (chairs):** Jordan Smoller and Melissa Haendel.
**Scope (per 2026 WG deck):** update the Scientific Roadmap reflecting
advances since 2023; re-examine the 8 focus-area framework; identify what's
changed in the science, the cohort, and the field; surface structural
changes to the framework as well as content-level priorities.

The program's current eight priority focus areas are: (1) Prevalent Common &
Rare Conditions; (2) Maternal & Child Health; (3) Healthy Aging & Resilience
Across the Lifespan; (4) Return of Results on Individual & Population Health;
(5) Lifestyle, Substance, & Behavioral Health; (6) Environment; (7) Health
Equity; (8) Genetics & Biology.

This is the **framework / focus-area need area** — recommendations about
focus-area structure, renames, and additions fall here, and should be
coordinated with the need areas they touch.

---

## Candidate recommendations evaluated

Pulled from the evidence base (gap_analysis §A/§B/§C/§D, crosswalk §4/§6,
temporal_trends §4–§7, emerging_themes §1–§5):

1. **Add a 9th focus area: "Data, AI & Methods"** (or similar) to absorb the
   theme-3 orphan (18% of pubs, 19% of projects) plus AI/foundation-models
   (HA4, 33 pubs) plus wearables (HA2, 75 pubs, mature/flat).
2. **Rename FA8 to "Genetics, Multi-omics & Biology"** to send a forward
   signal as the 2026 omics releases approach (HA1: 24 pubs today, peer
   median ~10% vs AoU 1.7%).
3. **Stand up a proteomics-data-release roadmap** (Olink/SomaScan-scale) as
   the largest peer-cohort growth differential (UKB 12.2% post-2023, AoU 1.8%).
4. **Rename FA2 "Maternal & Child Health" to "Maternal Health"** (or
   "Reproductive & Maternal Health") — "Child" content is structurally absent
   from an adult cohort (37 pubs all maternal-side).
5. **Investment in environmental-exposure data linkage** (PM2.5, EJI, heat,
   water) — largest single demand-supply gap (NIEHS 6 priorities, AoU 7 pubs).
6. **Materially elevate Aging/ADRD engagement** — AoU at 6.0% post-2023 (≈peer
   median, growing +4.7) while UKB (+6.9), MVP (+5.4), CKB (+5.1) grow on lecanemab/donanemab
   biobank work; AoU has structural advantage (EHR + UBR diversity) but
   under-utilization.
7. **Stool biospecimen / microbiome enablement** (NIDDK gut microbiome
   priority; HA7 11 pubs; peer median 11.1% vs AoU 0.8%).
8. **Reproducibility & data-quality framework** — formal commitment as the
   one of the six brainstorm questions; ties to NLM Goal 4 (sustainable
   infrastructure) and NIMHD G4 (methods/metrics).

The ranking that follows prioritizes **framework changes** (the core of this
need area), then **near-term feasibility × evidence weight**.

## Top-3 ranked recommendations

### 1. Add a 9th focus area: "Data, AI & Methods"

**Description.** Elevate methods/infrastructure/AI from the current orphan
status to a first-class focus area. Absorb theme 3 (262 pubs / 2,388 projects),
AI/foundation models (HA4: 33 pubs, 30 post-2023), wearables (HA2: 75 pubs,
already mature but currently invisible), and imaging-methods (HA3 will live
here too once imaging data flows). This is a framework-level change in this
need area, and the imaging, AI, and communications need areas all depend on
it for their work to have a coherent home.

**Supporting evidence.**
- Theme 3 is the *single largest orphan* in the crosswalk: ~18% of pubs / ~19%
  of projects with no home in the 8-area framework (`crosswalk_19_to_8.md`
  §4.1).
- HA4 (AI/foundation models) found 33 pubs (30 post-2023); examples
  PMID:39794311 (foundation-model phenome-wide onset prediction),
  PMID:40417537 (DL time-to-event for depression/asthma), PMID:40750633
  ("PAL-AI" model for oocyte maturation).
- HA2 (wearables) found 75 pubs (67 post-2023), trend flat — already mature
  but the only place to "park" them today is theme 7 (orphan) or stretch-fit
  into FA5. Slide 7 explicitly calls out "remote monitoring & digital health
  assessments."
- NLM strong-aligned, mission-explicitly names AoU; composite corpus weight
  223; FA-aligned theme 3 contributes 114.6 fractional pubs.
- AoU peer-index for theme 3 is 3.0× peer median — a *distinctive AoU
  contribution* that the 8-area framework hides.
- **External horizon scan (`../emerging-trends/emerging_trends_report.md`).** An
  open-discovery scan of 294k preprints + 423k NIH grants (2021-2026) ranks the
  two fastest-rising *AoU-feasible* white-space gaps in all of biomedicine as
  **genomic foundation models** and **LLMs/agentic AI** — exactly this focus
  area's content, with AoU <0.3% present in either. This is independent,
  field-level evidence that the framework is lagging where science is moving;
  it converts the orphan argument from "internal bookkeeping" to "AoU is absent
  from the literature's leading edge."

**Need area.** This falls under the Roadmap Refresh (framework) need area.
Both the AI need area (WG-4) and the imaging need area (WG-3) need a coherent
focus-area home for their recommendations to fit the larger Roadmap; without
the area, their work remains orphaned. Coordinate across these need areas.

**Rubric scores.**

| Criterion | Score | Justification |
|---|---:|---|
| Unmet researcher need | 3 | Researchers are *already doing* this work (theme 3 at 18% share). The unmet need is *recognition* in the framework, not the work itself. |
| Simplifies analysis | N/A | A focus-area change doesn't directly simplify analysis. |
| Standardizes/harmonizes | 4 | Naming a focus area drives standardization downstream (RFA topics, working-group structure, sci-com mapping). |
| Broad use cases | 4 | Methods/AI cross-cuts every disease area; this FA covers ≥18% of corpus by design. |
| Scientific Roadmap alignment | 4 | This is *the* Roadmap-Refresh recommendation — directly answers Q6. |
| Tool exists vs new dev | 4 | No new tools needed; this is a labeling/framework change. |
| No new data collection | 4 | Pure framework. |
| Helps with data quality | 3 | Indirect: elevating methods/QC as a first-class area legitimizes ongoing data-quality investment. |
| **Mean (applicable only)** | **3.71** | Excluding "Simplifies analysis" as N/A. |

**Risks / pushback expected.** Some may prefer reorganizing existing focus
areas over *adding* one; some may argue methods/AI are inherent to every FA
and don't need their own. Counter: the 18% corpus orphan is the empirical
answer; AI and imaging existing as distinct 2026 need areas is the structural
answer.

### 2. Rename FA8 to "Genetics, Multi-omics & Biology"; commit to a multi-omics-data-release roadmap

**Description.** Rename FA8 from "Genetics & Biology" to "Genetics,
Multi-omics & Biology" to send a forward signal that the 2026 omics releases
have a home in the framework. Pair the rename with a concrete commitment to
release proteomics (Olink-scale or SomaScan) and metabolomics on a stated
timeline — the rename without the data is a hollow gesture; the data without
the rename buries the work inside "genetics."

**Supporting evidence.**
- HA1 (multi-omics beyond genomics) is the *largest growth differential vs
  peers* in the entire analysis (`emerging_themes.md` §1). AoU 1.7% overall
  / 1.8% post-2023; UKB 12.2% post-2023; FinnGen 19.3%; CKB 6.2%.
- HA1: 24 pubs in AoU corpus, 23 of them post-2023, mostly using *external*
  proteomic/metabolomic data linked to AoU genotypes (PMID:41712304 COPD
  proteomics + PRS; PMID:40050615 multi-ancestry fibroid GWAS with
  eQTL/sQTL integration). The researcher base is already there; the data is
  not.
- NIDDK G1-RO1 (biological pathways & environmental contributors) + NHLBI
  Objective 4 (individual differences in pathobiology) both implicitly
  require multi-omics; ICs are demanding it.
- NHGRI Bold Prediction #2 (functional annotation of every human gene)
  implicitly requires the multi-omic toolkit.
- Crosswalk §4.3 documents the rationale: theme 2's definition is
  genomics-centric and FA8's name signals genetics-only.

**Need area.** Renaming a focus area is a framework matter in this need area;
the proteomics-release roadmap is shared with the DRC/program leadership, and
the Refresh is where the strategic priority is articulated. Coordinate with
the genetics/multi-omics need area.

**Rubric scores.**

| Criterion | Score | Justification |
|---|---:|---|
| Unmet researcher need | 4 | Largest peer-comparative growth differential — researchers want this data. |
| Simplifies analysis | 3 | A multi-omic data release simplifies what's currently a multi-step external-linkage workflow. |
| Standardizes/harmonizes | 4 | Olink/SomaScan platforms have established assay and QC standards; AoU adopting them harmonizes with UKB-PPP. |
| Broad use cases | 4 | Multi-omics underpins disease mechanism, drug response, PRS refinement, biomarker discovery — pan-FA. |
| Scientific Roadmap alignment | 4 | Slide 7 names multi-omics beyond genomics as a 2023+ priority. |
| Tool exists vs new dev | 3 | Olink and SomaScan are existing platforms; analysis tools exist via UKB-PPP. Integration with AoU Workbench is the new work. |
| No new data collection | 2 | Proteomics requires new assays on existing blood samples — not new participant collection but new data generation. |
| Helps with data quality | 3 | Multi-omic data improves phenotype validation and biomarker triangulation. |
| **Mean (applicable only)** | **3.38** | |

**Risks / pushback expected.** "What does multi-omics cost?" is the obvious
question — the WG should hand the cost question to DRC/leadership but the
strategic prioritization is in the Refresh's gift. Some Sci Com members may
push back on the rename as cosmetic without the data investment; the
recommendation should explicitly pair the two.

### 3. Reframe FA4 toward implementation; reconsider FA2 naming; add an environmental-exposure linkage commitment under FA6

**Description.** Three smaller framework-level corrections, packaged as one
structural-cleanup recommendation: (a)
re-scope FA4 "Return of Results" from *ELSI research* (which is maturing
out — theme 17 trend −12.4) to *ROR implementation* (PGx workflows, ACMG
delivery, pharmacy integration); (b) rename FA2 "Maternal & Child Health"
to "Maternal Health" (or "Reproductive & Maternal Health") to acknowledge
that AoU is an adult cohort and the "Child" content is aspirational; (c)
add an explicit FA6 commitment to environmental-exposure data linkage
(PM2.5, EJI, heat exposure, water quality) — the largest demand-supply gap
in the entire IC alignment matrix.

**Supporting evidence.**
- FA4 trend −9.8 as share of corpus; theme 17 (ELSI) is the largest single
  theme decline (−12.4) — the program-introduction-and-recruitment-methodology
  wave has crested. Substantive PGx and ACMG ROR work continues (theme 10
  rare disease +1.6; theme 16 PGx +0.5) — these are *implementation* work,
  not ELSI research (`temporal_trends.md` §2; crosswalk §5).
- FA2 currently 2.7% of pubs, 5.3% of projects — entirely maternal-side; the
  "Child" component is aspirational rather than evidenced (crosswalk §5).
- NIEHS has 6 plan priorities tagged to T19; AoU corpus has 7 substantive
  T19 pubs (gap_analysis §B1). NIEHS-T1 (foundational), NIEHS-T2 (climate &
  health), NIEHS-T4 (exposome) all explicitly call for human-cohort
  exposure linkage; AoU is the natural pan-IC delivery vehicle if the
  linkage exists.
- NCCIH O3 (Whole Person Health, environmental domain) + NHLBI crosscutting
  Lifestyle theme also depend on FA6 substance.

**Need area.** All three are framework-level changes in the Roadmap Refresh
need area. Bundling protects the overall recommendation budget — three small
structural fixes consume one of the three top-recommendation slots.

**Rubric scores.**

| Criterion | Score | Justification |
|---|---:|---|
| Unmet researcher need | 3 | Environmental piece scores 4; FA2/FA4 renames are framework hygiene (lower direct researcher impact). Average 3. |
| Simplifies analysis | 2 | FA4 reframe slightly simplifies (signals what work to expect); the renames don't directly. |
| Standardizes/harmonizes | 3 | Environmental-exposure linkage standardizes geocoded-exposure access; FA renames standardize framework language. |
| Broad use cases | 3 | Environmental linkage broadens use cases; the FA renames are narrower. |
| Scientific Roadmap alignment | 4 | Three changes the Roadmap should make per the evidence. |
| Tool exists vs new dev | 3 | Environmental linkage uses existing EPA / CDC data and existing geocoding pipelines; AoU integration is the work. |
| No new data collection | 3 | No new participant data; environmental linkage is *external* data integration. |
| Helps with data quality | 3 | Geocoded exposure linkage improves environmental-phenotype validity; FA2/FA4 renames marginal. |
| **Mean (applicable only)** | **3.00** | |

**Risks / pushback expected.** FA2 rename is politically sensitive — some
members may read it as deprioritizing pediatric science; the framing should
emphasize accuracy (AoU is structurally adult; "Child" reads as a false
promise) rather than priority. Environmental linkage will draw "what does it
cost / who owns it" — the answer is "external public datasets + AoU geocoding;
the question is engineering, not data acquisition." FA4 reframe is the
lowest-risk piece.

## Also-considered (deprioritized with reasoning)

- **Aging/ADRD engagement (candidate 6).** Strong evidence (AoU 6.0% post-2023, ≈peer median, growing +4.7
  vs UKB +6.9, MVP +5.4, CKB +5.1 post-2023; lecanemab/donanemab era;
  emerging_themes.md §5). Deprioritized to "also-considered" because it's a
  *researcher-outreach / engagement* recommendation, not a framework or
  structural change — better held by WG-5 Communications or a downstream
  NIA-engagement subgroup. Worth surfacing in cross-cutting synthesis.
- **Stool biospecimen / microbiome (candidate 7).** Real gap (peer median
  11.1% vs AoU 0.8%) but requires fundamental new sample collection at the
  cohort level — a multi-year ask that the WG can flag but not deliver in a
  Refresh. Deprioritized because it doesn't fit the "near-term Refresh" frame.
- **Reproducibility & data-quality framework (candidate 8).** Excellent
  brainstorm topic but more naturally a WG-2 (Translational) or WG-5
  (Communications, impact-tracking) deliverable. Surfaced in cross-cutting.
- **Subcontinental ancestry framing (gap_analysis §C5).** A *latent
  strength* (PMID:40480197) where AoU is ahead of IC plan articulation but
  not a Refresh framework change. Belongs in defensive narrative.
