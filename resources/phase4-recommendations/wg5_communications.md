# WG-5: Scientific Communications

> This is a **topic / need area**, not a decision body. Its topic overlaps
> with the other need areas and should be coordinated across them. The WG
> label is used here as a topic reference.

## Lead(s) and scope

**Lead (chair):** Korf.
**Scope (per 2026 WG deck):** dissemination of AoU science + measurable
impact tracking; Sci Com ambassador toolkit (per slide 12); audience-defined
communications products.

This need area converts the Refresh framework (WG-1), the translational
metrics (WG-2), and the distinctive evidence the corpus shows into
externally-legible products and narratives. It is the need area most directly
tied to the Sci Com's own visibility and credibility. Coordinate across the
need areas it draws on.

---

## Candidate recommendations evaluated

1. **An impact-tracking framework** built on WG-2's translational metrics
   plus citation- and policy-based metrics — annual reporting product.
2. **3–5 audience-defined dissemination products** (researcher-facing,
   IC-program-officer-facing, Congressional-facing, patient/participant-
   facing, journalist-facing).
3. **A plain-language equity narrative** — the strongest defensive
   evidence (ELSI 21×, SDOH 5.1×, PGx 4.6× peer-over-index) packaged for
   external audiences.
4. **Sci Com ambassador toolkit** (per slide 12 of the WG deck) — slide
   decks, talking points, FAQ, briefing-paper template.
5. **A researcher-engagement-channel metric** (Workbench user growth,
   retention, days-to-first-result, time-to-first-pub).
6. **Refreshed "what AoU is for" externally-facing statement** —
   distillation of the distinctive-strengths argument.

## Top-3 ranked recommendations

### 1. Define an impact-tracking framework and publish an annual AoU Impact Report

**Description.** Establish an annual AoU Impact Report that operationalizes
WG-2's TSBM metrics plus citation-based, policy-based, and engagement
metrics. The Report is the **single externally-citable artifact** that
captures translational, scientific, equity, and engagement performance —
each year. Produce v1 within 12 months; publish on the program site and
distribute to IC program officers, ACD, Congressional staff, and
participant communities.

**Supporting evidence.**
- WG-2 Recommendation 1 produces the metric definitions; WG-5 produces
  the reporting product. The two are tightly coupled.
- HA8 breakthrough therapeutics (20 pubs, 19 post-2023) and HA4 AI/foundation
  models (33 pubs, 30 post-2023) are exactly the kinds of high-impact
  outputs that *vanish* from program-level conversation without an
  Impact Report aggregating them.
- The defensive narrative for AoU's funding case rests on quantified
  impact (per cross-cutting synthesis). An annual Report turns each
  year's evidence into something Congressional/IC stakeholders can cite.
- NLM-G2 (dissemination and engagement) + NLM-COHORTS (the named AoU
  alignment).
- Precedent: NIH BD2K, the BRAIN Initiative, Cancer Moonshot all publish
  annual progress / impact products.

**Need area.** Impact aggregation and external reporting falls under the
communications need area.

**Risks / pushback expected.** "Who curates it?" — the WG should specify a
DRC + program-leadership co-curated model. "What about negative findings?" —
honest reporting (e.g., named gaps in HA3 imaging, HA5 environment) is
*more credible* than triumphalist reporting; the long-doc guidance about
"factual correctness > comprehensiveness" applies.

### 2. Build the plain-language distinctive-evidence narrative ("What AoU does that no other cohort does")

**Description.** Develop a concise, audience-tunable narrative anchored on
the **three quantified distinctive strengths** the corpus analysis has
surfaced: (1) **ELSI / participant engagement (26.3× peer median)** — AoU
is the program that figured out how to run participant-as-partner research
at scale; (2) **SDOH / disparities (5.1× peer median)** — AoU is the only
cohort that *built diversity into its sampling frame*; (3) **PGx (4.6×
peer median)** — AoU is the cohort that uniquely advances
implementation-grade pharmacogenomics. Pair with methods-infrastructure
(3.0× peer median) as a fourth supporting strength. This narrative is
the **foundation** of the ambassador toolkit, the Impact Report frame, and
the IC-program-officer briefings.

**Supporting evidence.**
- `aou_vs_peers_index.csv` (peer index data): ELSI 26.28; Theme 6 SDOH
  5.141; PGx 4.601; Theme 3 methods/infrastructure 3.018; Theme 9
  autoimmune 2.44; Theme 16 PGx 4.601. Numerical evidence is precise.
- ELSI: theme 17, AoU 4.9% / peer 0.2%. The number speaks for itself.
- SDOH: theme 6, AoU 32.9% / peer 6.5%. NIMHD G7 *literally describes* the
  AoU mandate.
- PGx: theme 16, AoU 2.4% / peer 0.5%. NIGMS-G4 (PGRN) + NCATS Translator.
- Methods: theme 3, AoU 18.3% / peer 6.1%. NLM mission-explicit alignment.
- The PMI 2015 founding report and NIH-Wide CCT1 (Minority Health) and
  CCT4 (Collaborative Science) provide the long-term narrative through-
  line — AoU is delivering on its founding promise.

**Need area.** Narrative work falls under the communications need area. The
quantified peer-index evidence comes from the Refresh corpus analysis;
turning numbers into narrative sits in this need area.

**Risks / pushback expected.** Sci Com may push to add more distinctive

indexes 0.5–1.4×). Distinctive ≠ growing. The headline rests on the four
genuine over-indexes. "Dermatology at 6.9×" will come up — the WG must
have the **artifact-flag answer rehearsed** (1–2 prolific groups; declining
trend; not strategic).

### 3. Audience-tuned dissemination products (5 named, with ambassador toolkit)

**Description.** Define and produce five audience-tuned dissemination
products that the Sci Com can deploy: (a) **researcher-facing** —
quarterly Workbench Highlights digest; (b) **IC-program-officer-facing**
— per-IC briefs (already drafted, 21 in `aou-ic-alignment/per_ic_briefs/`
— productize them); (c) **Congressional/government-affairs** — a
2-page leverage-and-distinctive-contribution brief, plain-English; (d)
**patient/participant-facing** — annual participant-impact report; (e)
**journalist / public-information** — story-pitch sheets paired with each
year's Impact Report. Bundle with the Sci Com ambassador toolkit
(slide deck, FAQ, talking points) per slide 12 of the WG deck.

**Supporting evidence.**
- `per_ic_briefs/` directory contains 21 one-page IC briefs ready to be
  productized — the engineering is mostly done.
- HA8 breakthrough therapeutics (PMID:41847743 GLP-1 hepatic
  decompensation, PMID:41807852 CRC cost-effectiveness) are exactly the
  kinds of stories that translate cleanly to Congressional and journalist
  audiences.
- Subcontinental ancestry work (PMID:40480197) is a story that fits
  patient-facing and journalist-facing channels.
- NLM-G2 (dissemination & engagement) + NHGRI-A4 (guiding principles &
  values, including societal stewardship).
- Cross-cutting NIH Theme 4 (Collaborative Science) reinforces the
  multi-audience approach.

**Need area.** Dissemination products and the ambassador toolkit fall under
the communications need area (explicit slide-12 topics).

**Risks / pushback expected.** Resource ask — five products is real
sustained work. Defend by tying to existing artifacts (per-IC briefs
exist; Impact Report from Rec 1 carries journalist + Congressional
audiences). "Why not more?" — focus is the point; five well-maintained
products beat ten that drift out of date.
